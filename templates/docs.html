    Getting started

    Create your account

    1. 1 Press Launch app, then Create an account (or Start free under Plans on the home page).
    2. 2 Type your email and a password of at least 8 characters. If the page shows a check that you are not a robot, tick it.
    3. 3 Open the email we send and press Confirm my account on the page it opens.
    4. 4 Sign in.

    Confirm within {{ verify_minutes }} minutes. After that the unconfirmed account is removed and you can simply sign up again.

    Opening the link alone does nothing: you must press the button. This stops company mail scanners, which open every link, from confirming accounts nobody asked for.

    Sign in and passwords

    • Remember me keeps this browser signed in for {{ remember_days }} days.
    • Forgot password? on the sign in page emails you a link to choose a new one. It works for {{ reset_minutes }} minutes. Setting the new password signs out every device; then sign in with it.
    • After {{ fail_limit }} wrong passwords in {{ limit_minutes }} minutes, signing in pauses and the page says how many minutes to wait. A browser you signed in with before has its own count, so strangers guessing your password cannot lock you out there.
    • New passwords that appear in known data breaches are refused.

    Years and your base

    Nomad Life counts in solar years, 1 January to 31 December. Each year is its own workspace with a base: the city and country you live in, usually where you pay tax.

    Create a year

    Your first sign in opens Set up your year. Later years start from the + Year tab on the dashboard.

    {{ shot('year-new', 'The Set up your year form with Year 2027, Base country Portugal and Base city Lisbon filled in', 'Example: 2027 with the base in Lisbon, Portugal.') }}

    You can have one workspace per year. The tabs next to + Year switch between them.

    Change your base or delete a year

    On the dashboard, the gear at the top right of the Base card, Manage Base country, lets you change the base city and country, upload your rental contract, or delete the year. Deleting a year removes its movements and receipts and asks you to type the year to confirm.

    Movements

    A movement is a stay in one country: a trip, a month in another city, or your time at the base (only logged days count). Log each one with its country, dates and, if you like, the city.

    Add a movement

    {{ shot('movement-new', 'The New movement form: after typing "ita" in Country, the list offers Italy; numbered markers point at Country, the To date and the Add movement button', '1 Pick the country, 2 set the dates, 3 press Add movement. A receipt can be attached right away.') }}
    • Press + Add movement on the dashboard.
    • The country is what counts; the city is optional. Without one, the movement shows as its country, such as Italy.
    • Pick From and To in the calendar that opens on each date (an empty To opens on the month of From). Both dates count. From 1 March to 3 March is 3 days.
    • The dates must fall inside the year. A trip over New Year is two movements: 28 to 31 December in one year, 1 to 5 January in the next.
    • Notes are optional, up to {{ '{:,}'.format(notes_max) }} characters, and only you and the delegates you invite see them (the accountant package leaves them out unless you ask).

    The country picker

    Type the start of a name or of one of its words, a two letter code or a common name: ita finds Italy, JP finds Japan, Holland finds Netherlands (Holland). Accents do not matter. Use the arrow keys and Enter, or tap a country.

    • Your year's base country comes first, with a blue home icon.
    • Favorites: press the star next to a country (or Shift+Enter on the highlighted one) to keep it in the Favorites tab, next to All countries; Alt+→ and Alt+← switch between them. Favorites are saved with your account, on every device, up to {{ favorites_max }}.

    A place that is not in the list can be typed as it is. It is counted as its own country and listed under the map.

    Edit or delete a movement

    Press the place in the Movements table, or Edit. Delete removes the movement and its receipts after two confirmations.

    {{ shot('delete-dialog', 'The delete confirmation dialog on step 2 of 2: "Are you sure? This permanently deletes the movement to Madrid and its 2 receipts." with Cancel and Yes, delete permanently', 'Every delete asks twice. The second step says what will be removed.') }}

    Limits: city and country up to {{ place_max }} characters, and up to {{ '{:,}'.format(movements_max) }} movements a year.

    Movements from Google Calendar

    {{ shot('sync-switch', 'The head of the dashboard: the year 2025 with an AUTO label and a switch turned on next to it, the year tabs on the right', 'AUTO: the year follows your Google calendar. The switch turns it back to MANUAL.') }}

    Each year is MANUAL or AUTO, shown next to the year on the dashboard. A new year starts in MANUAL: you add its movements yourself. In AUTO, its movements come from the Google calendar you connected (see Calendar integration), and they follow it each time you open the year, also right after you sign in.

    • Events count when their title is a place: a country, by name or code (Italy, IT), a city and its country (Rome, Italy), or a city and its US state, Canadian province or Australian state (Austin, Texas, Toronto, ON). A state code that is also a country's, as in Chicago, IL (Israel), is not guessed: write Chicago, USA. Other events, like a dentist appointment, are left out. An all-day event from 1 to 6 February gives a stay from 1 to 5 February, as Google shows it; days outside the year are cut.
    • Google Calendar wins: change a trip there and its movement changes here; delete it there and the movement goes, unless it has receipts. Then it stays, marked Not in Google Calendar, until you delete it yourself.
    • Deleting a movement in AUTO deletes its event in Google Calendar too, after the usual two confirmations (otherwise the next sync would bring it back). In MANUAL, a movement that came from an event offers it as a choice in the second confirmation, unticked. The first time, Google asks you once to let Nomad Life change your calendar: allow it, then press Delete again. Nomad Life never changes anything else in Google Calendar, and a movement you typed yourself has no event to delete.
    • In AUTO, a movement's country and dates come from its event: change them in Google Calendar. So does its city when the event names one (“Rome, Italy”); when it names only a country (“Italy”), you can type a city on the movement's page, and it stays in Nomad Life only. Its notes and receipts are yours and stay. + Add movement is hidden: add the trip in Google Calendar. Movements you typed before turning AUTO on and chose to keep stay as they are.
    • The Refresh Calendar arrow of the Google Calendar tab reads the calendar again at once; otherwise Nomad Life reads it at most once a minute.
    • If Google Calendar does not answer, nothing changes and the dashboard says so. If it is disconnected, another calendar is chosen, the calendar is deleted, or Google takes the access back, every AUTO year goes back to MANUAL at once and keeps its movements, with a message that says why: turn AUTO on again to review the differences. AUTO never comes back by itself.
    • A year holds at most {{ '{:,}'.format(movements_max) }} movements: events past that are left out, and a note under the year says how many.
    • A calendar with more events than Nomad Life can read at once never loses movements: only additions and changes apply until all of it can be read.

    Turning AUTO on

    {{ shot('sync-review', 'The Turn on AUTO page: changes from Google Calendar grouped in Change and Remove, each with a checkbox, Select all and Select none, and the Turn on AUTO and apply and Cancel buttons', 'Before AUTO starts, you choose which differences to apply.') }}

    Press the switch next to the year. A page lists what would change for the movements to match your Google calendar: movements to add, to change (the same country on overlapping days takes the event's dates, and its city when the event names one; a city you typed stays when the event names only the country), to remove (with their receipts), and those already matching. Movements already linked to events of another calendar, or to events made again, are matched like typed ones, so their notes and receipts stay. Untick what you do not want: an unticked event stays out of the sync, and the movement stays as you typed it. Removing a movement that has receipts starts unticked. Press Turn on AUTO and apply, or Cancel to stay in MANUAL. Switching back to MANUAL keeps every movement as it is.

    How days are counted

    Every day of the year goes to one country at most. Four rules decide which one:

    1. Movements count in full, planned future ones too.
    2. Only logged days count. A day without a movement counts for no country, past or future. To count days at your base, add a movement there too (for example your base city for the months you lived there).
    3. A day in two movements (an overlap), such as a travel day, counts for the one that started later. If both start the same day, the shorter one wins; with identical dates, the newest.
    4. A movement inside your base country counts for the base country.

    When movements overlap by more than a travel day (one ends the day the next begins), a red triangle blinks next to the Movements title (until you open its report). Press it to read which movements share which days and where those days count, copy the report, and fix the dates if you were not in both places.

    A worked example

    Base Lisbon, Portugal. Lisbon logged from 1 to 3 March, Spain from 3 to 6 March, Italy from 6 to 8 March, and a trip to Thailand planned for 13 and 14 March.

    The {{ threshold }} day line

    Many countries treat you as a tax resident once you spend {{ threshold }} days or more there in a year. The Days in Base country card marks that line on its bar: with the marker on, point at the bar to read At least {{ threshold }} days, or how many days you are short. In Days per country a bar turns red when another country reaches {{ threshold }} days.

    Nomad Life counts days; it does not give tax advice. Rules differ by country (some count partial days or look at other ties), so check yours with an accountant. The guides on the {{ threshold }} day rule and counting your days explain more.

    Receipts

    Keep the proof of where you were: rental contracts, hotel bills, flight tickets.

    Upload a receipt

    {{ shot('receipts', 'The Receipts card of a movement: a type menu set to Flight ticket, a chosen file named flight-lis-bcn.pdf and the Upload button, below two saved receipts', 'Choose the type and the file, then press Upload.') }}
    • Upload on a movement's page, or on the base page for your rental contract.
    • Types: {{ kinds() }}, chosen from a list with an icon for each.
    • Files: PDF, PNG, JPG, HEIC or WebP. Files are checked by their content, so a web page renamed to .pdf is refused.
    • Big JPG, PNG and WebP photos (over {{ photo_megapixels }} megapixels or {{ photo_shrink_mb }} MB) are resized to at most about {{ photo_megapixels }} megapixels and saved as JPEG without their metadata, which removes the location a phone stores in a photo. They are never made narrower than {{ '{:,}'.format(photo_min_side) }} pixels, so long screenshots stay readable. HEIC files, smaller photos and photos over {{ photo_max_megapixels }} megapixels are stored as they are, metadata included.

    Settings

    Open Settings from the menu under your name.

    Name, password and email

    • Profile: your first and last name, shown in the menu, in emails and on the accountant package.
    • Change password: needs your current password. You stay signed in here; other devices are signed out.
    • Change email: we send a link to the new address, valid {{ email_change_minutes }} minutes, and nothing changes until you open it and press Confirm my new email. Your old address gets an alert with an Undo this change link, valid {{ revert_days }} days, in case it was not you. The link still works if the address is changed again meanwhile.

    Calendar integration

    • Calendar Integration connects one Google Calendar: its events show in the dashboard's Google Calendar tab, and a year in AUTO takes its movements from it (see Movements from Google Calendar). Open it from Settings, or with the gear next to the title of the dashboard's Calendar.
    • Press Connect Google Calendar, choose the Google account (Google always asks, also when you are signed in to one) and sign in. Google is asked for the list of your calendars and to see their events, read only, for the dashboard's Google Calendar tab: Nomad Life never changes your events and does not store them.
    • Connected before the events could be shown? The Google Calendar tab asks for that one permission: press Allow events, and Google brings you back to the tab. Your chosen calendar stays chosen.
    • Back in Settings, choose one calendar and press Save calendar. Only calendars you can edit are listed, your main one first.
    • The chosen calendar then shows as Connected. Its Disconnect button lets you choose another one; your Google account stays connected.
    • While no calendar is chosen, Refresh list reads your calendars again, for example after creating one in Google Calendar. Disconnect Google account removes the connection and Nomad Life's access to your Google account; deleting your account does the same.
    • If you remove Nomad Life's access in your Google account, the section says so: press Connect again.

    Delegates

    {{ shot('delegates', 'The Delegates card in Settings: an accountant who can view the account, with the day they accepted and last viewed it, an invitation still waiting with Send again, a Remove button on each, and the form to invite someone with their email and your password', 'Invite someone with their email and your password; Remove ends their access at once.') }}

    A delegate is someone who can view your account, such as your accountant or your partner: your years, the dashboard, your movements and notes, your receipts and base documents, and the accountant package when your plan has it. A delegate cannot change anything, and never sees your Google Calendar or your settings.

    1. In Settings, under Delegates, type the email address of the person and your password, and press Invite. You can have up to {{ delegates_max }} delegates, invitations included.
    2. They get an email. They sign in with that address (or first create a free account with it), open Shared with you in the menu at the top (a red dot on their email says an invitation waits, and their own dashboard says so too), and press Accept. You get an email when they do.
    3. From then on, Shared with you lists your account for them: Open shows it read only, with a note at the top saying whose account it is and Switch back to their own. Their dashboard also has View shared calendar, straight to your calendar.

    Your list shows each delegate with the day they accepted and the last day they viewed your account. Send again emails a waiting invitation once more, and Remove ends their access at once. A delegate can also leave with Stop viewing. Undoing an email change from the old address removes every delegate too, in case whoever changed it invited themselves.

    Two-factor sign in

    {{ shot('two-factor', 'The Two-factor sign in card: a Passkeys list with two passkeys, each with Rename and Remove, an Add a passkey form, and below it the Authenticator app setup with a QR code (blurred here), three steps and the Add authenticator app button', 'Add a passkey for each device, or scan the code with an authenticator app, or both.') }}

    After your password, signing in can also ask for a passkey or a code from an authenticator app. Set up either one, or both: the sign in page then offers both.

    Passkey (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello, a security key, or a password manager such as iCloud Keychain, Google Password Manager or 1Password): under Passkeys, give it a name if you like, type your password and press Add a passkey, then confirm on your device. You can add up to {{ passkeys_max }}, one for each device, and rename or remove them there.

    Authenticator app:

    1. In an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password, Bitwarden) add an account by scanning the QR code, or type the setup key.
    2. Type the 6 digit code the app shows, and your password.
    3. Press Turn on two-factor sign in.

    Two-factor sign in stays on while you have a passkey or an authenticator app. Lost your phone or passkey? See Troubleshooting.

    Devices and activity

    Troubleshooting

    No confirmation email, or the link expired

    Check your spam folder. Signing in with your email and password sends a new link. Emails go out at most once a minute per account. If {{ verify_minutes }} minutes have passed, sign up again with the same address.

    "Too many failed sign in attempts"

    Wait the minutes the message says, then try again, or use Forgot password?. The pause protects your account from password guessing.

    Lost the phone with your authenticator app

    If you set up both a passkey and an authenticator app, use the one you still have. Lost them all? Write to {{ support_email }} from your account's email address. Once we have checked it is you, we remove two-factor sign in (passkeys and app) so you can sign in with your password and set it up again.

    A receipt is refused

    Only PDF, PNG, JPG, HEIC and WebP files are accepted, up to the per receipt size of your plan. Export other formats (Word, Pages, a web page) to PDF first. If the message says your storage is full, delete old receipts or upgrade.

    The day count looks wrong

    Look for "counted" under a movement in the table: it shares days with another one. Remember that a day without a movement counts for no country, past or future (your base too: log your time there as a movement), and that both the first and the last day of a movement count. See How days are counted.
